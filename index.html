THE DECISION SHIFT METHOD

Six moves for
a stalled decision.

Use the sequence to find what’s holding up the decision, compare the options using evidence, and arrive at a next step the other person can evaluate.

Diagnose

  • D1

    Desired outcome

    Define what needs to change in the other person’s work.

  • D2

    Decision friction

    Find the question, risk, or constraint weighing on the choice.

Reframe

  • S1

    Cost of waiting

    Compare the cost of waiting with the cost of acting.

  • S2

    Reframing

    Bring the missing information into the comparison.

Move forward

  • M1

    Narrative immersion

    Show the consequences in a situation the buyer recognizes.

  • M2

    Logical next step

    Agree on a step that fits what you know so far.

A QUESTION TO START WITH

Which part of the proposal do you still need to evaluate?

The answer may reveal a specific concern. If the buyer wants to end the conversation, respect that decision.

Is the price above your budget, or do you have a question about what it covers?

A firm budget calls for a conversation about price and scope. A question about delivery calls for evidence.

Would you like to leave it here, or revisit it when something changes?

If they want to revisit it, confirm what needs to change and ask whether you can follow up.

Chapter 3 · A fictional example from the book

“I need to think about it,” Rafael says. The vendor asks whether a discount would help. Rafael has yet to reach the price on page two. He says the vendor can email the new terms. Two versions of the proposal now sit in his inbox, and he still has the same question.

At the next meeting, the vendor asks what Rafael needs to know to evaluate the test. Rafael opens a screen from a previous rollout and points to a list of discrepancies that were closed on paper and still open in the warehouse. He wants to know who will fix them and how much work falls to his team. A discount might affect the financial case. The question uncovered a condition the project would have to meet before the team could weigh that case.
